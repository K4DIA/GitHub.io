Projects / Common-mode choke

RF / Feed line Built

1:1 common-mode choke
on an FT240 core

An attic loop fed through an unbalanced tuner puts RF on the outside of the coax, and from there onto everything else in the house. Twelve turns of RG-58 on a ferrite ring, in a weatherproof box, mounted right at the tuner. Nothing clever, just wound properly and put in the right place.

Range
80 through 10 m
Power
120 W PEP
Core
FT240-31 or FT240-43, 2.4 in OD
Winding
12 turns RG-58, crossed over
Connectors
Two N-type female
Feeding
Attic loop through an Icom AH-4
RF hot

The AH-4 chassis carries RF. Its ground terminal connects to the loop end, which makes the case live at RF while you are transmitting. Isolate it from joists and sheathing with dry plastic standoffs or hang it on ties, and do not handle it with the transmitter keyed.

The part that matters

Winding the core

Twelve turns
Crossed over at the halfway point

The cross-over is the whole trick. Winding straight round the ring leaves both tails together at one point, which couples input to output and undoes the work. Winding half the turns one way, crossing over, and winding the other half back gives you the same twelve turns with the tails at opposite ends of the core.

Into the box

Assembly

Drilling

  1. Mark the centres for the two jacks on opposite walls.
  2. Drill the barrel clearance holes and the four screw holes for each.
  3. Drill two 1/8 in holes through the backplate for the anchor tie.

Mounting

  1. Fit both connectors with stainless hardware.
  2. Put a grounding lug under one mounting nut at each connector, inside the box.
  3. Trim the coax tails so they reach the pins without tension.

Soldering

  1. Strip 1/2 in of outer jacket from each tail.
  2. Unbraid the shield and twist it into a single lead.
  3. Strip 1/4 in of the inner insulation.
  4. Centre conductor into the connector centre cup, exposed run as short as you can make it.
  5. Shield lead to the grounding lug.
  6. Zip tie the core down through the backplate holes.

Where it goes

Installed position

At the tuner
Not at the radio

A choke works where the common-mode current is, which is at the feedpoint. Two to four feet from the AH-4 input. Putting it down at the shack end leaves the whole run between tuner and radio radiating.

Worth knowing

Limits and checks

  • Rated here for 120 W PEP across 80 through 10 m. Duty cycle matters: a long digital transmission heats a core that an SSB conversation never would.
  • Mix 31 and Mix 43 behave differently across the band. Both are named in the parts list because either will work over this range, not because they are interchangeable everywhere.
  • A choke does not fix a bad ground or a badly placed antenna. It stops common-mode current riding the outside of the coax; that is all it does.
  • RG-400 is the substitution for a hot attic, where RG-58 jacket can soften.

Built for one specific station: an attic loop fed by an Icom AH-4 into an unbalanced tuner port. The winding and the box carry over to any similar installation, but the placement advice is written for that arrangement.
