Prototype, Pi bench test next

Speak English.
They hear Italian.

The Offline Radio Translator is a Raspberry Pi that sits between your headset and your radio. You talk, it transmits what you said in Italian, Japanese or any of 27 other languages, and it puts the reply in your headphones in English. No internet, no cloud, no phone held up to a microphone.

Take it, read it, break it

The whole project is on GitHub under the MIT license: one Python tool, the installer, the operating guide and 105 automated tests. It has not run on a Raspberry Pi with the real models yet, and that is why it is public now. Clone it, run the tests, read the reviewer notes, and tell me what I got wrong.

It started as a what-if on Discord

The idea is David's
The build is mine

The idea is not mine. In October 2026, in a ham radio Discord channel, David Eisenhardt, W4DPE, was explaining where he draws the line on AI in the hobby. He used a translator as his example. You talk into a microphone plugged into a Pi 5, it turns your English into Italian and speaks it over the air, and you work phone with that much help and no more. He said he would be fine with that.

He had two worries. How much power does it burn, and does it only work because it was built on other people's work, taken without asking? A phone can already do most of this, he said, but it leans on somebody else's servers and it sounds bad held up to a radio microphone. He was looking for ground that both camps could stand on: the operators who will try anything new, and the ones who get called luddites for having real objections. If it ran locally on prebuilt libraries, at the wattage of a Pi, he called that a limit worth having.

I read that and said to myself, this does not need a large language model. That is an axe to slice a steak. The work is three small jobs in a row: turn speech into text, translate the text, speak the result. Each one has a small open model made for exactly that job, and all three fit on a Pi. They are still neural networks. They are just the right size for the work.

So I built it inside the limits he set.

Three things it will not do

Offline
Operator in control

Send your voice to a server

Speech recognition, translation and the synthetic voice all run on the board. It needs the internet once, to install. After that it works in a field with no signal bars.

Translate a callsign

Callsigns, signal reports, Q-codes, grid squares and frequencies are pulled out before translation and put back exactly. K1ABC stays K1ABC, and five nine stays five nine.

Key the radio on its own

The transmitter keys only after your own PTT press and a second press to confirm what is about to go out. No timer and no network request can key it.

From PTT to their speaker

Half-duplex
It translates between overs

Phone-mode radio is half-duplex: you talk, say "over", and the other station waits. So the box never has to translate while you speak. It records the whole over, translates it, then keys up. The processing time looks like a slow "over". Times on the left are Pi 4 estimates.

Transmit: you to the other station

  1. 0 s1
    YouPress PTT

    Hold the footswitch, or press Enter. This only tells the Pi to listen; the radio is not keyed.

  2. talking2
    YouSpeak English into the headset

    "Good morning K1ABC, you're five nine, thanks for the contact."

  3. release3
    YouRelease PTT

    Let go of the footswitch, or press Enter again. Recording stops (45 s limit).

  4. +3 to 7 s4
    PiSpeech to text

    whisper.cpp turns your voice into English text, on the Pi.

  5. 5
    PiJargon guard

    Callsigns, signal reports, Q-codes, grids and frequencies are set aside so the translator can't change them.

  6. +0.5 to 2 s6
    PiTranslate

    Argos Translate language pack, English → Italian. The jargon is put back exactly.

  7. +1.5 to 3 s7
    PiSynthesized voice

    Piper (or Open JTalk for Japanese) speaks the Italian. Callsigns come out in phonetics.

  8. your call8
    YouReview, then press again to send

    The screen shows what you said and what will go out. Press Enter to transmit. Anything else, or no answer in 20 s, discards it.

  9. keyed9
    RadioPi keys the transmitter

    CAT command through rigctld, the interface's RTS line, or VOX.

  10. on air10
    RadioDisclosure, message, station ID

    "Trasmissione tradotta automaticamente." then the Italian, then your call in English when an ID is due. A long message is cut to fit 90 s; the ID never is.

  11. ≤ 90 s11
    RadioUnkey

    Always: after an error, if the program is killed, or if the sound device stalls (a watchdog unkeys).

Receive: the other station to you

  1. they talk1
    RadioTheir Italian comes in

    Audio from the rig into the USB interface.

  2. silence2
    PiFind the over

    Signal level marks where they start and stop. Bursts of noise are ignored.

  3. 3
    PiSpeech to text, in Italian

    Known Whisper noise phrases are filtered out.

  4. 4
    PiTranslate Italian → English

    Same jargon guard on the way back.

  5. a few s5
    YouEnglish in your headphones

    The screen shows their original words beside the translation.

Receive is muted while you transmit, so the Pi never translates its own signal.

What the other station hears

Disclosure first
Your ID in English

One over, as it goes out
  1. Trasmissione tradotta automaticamente."Machine-translated transmission." Said first, in their language, on every over.
  2. Buongiorno Kilo 1 Alfa Bravo Charlie, sei 5 9, grazie per il collegamento.Your message. The callsign and signal report came through translation untouched.
  3. This is Whiskey 4 Delta Papa Echo.English ID (FCC §97.119): added to the end of your first over, and to any over that would otherwise end more than 10 minutes after the last ID. A recording of your own voice works too.

The other station needs nothing special. They hear a synthetic Italian voice and reply normally.

English plus 29 languages

Both directions
Each with an offline voice

Each one has a translation pack in both directions with English, Whisper speech recognition, an offline voice, and its own spoken disclosure line (shown on each card).

Japanese uses Open JTalk because Piper has no Japanese voice. Callsign phonetics are spoken in katakana (キロ 1 アルファ…), since Open JTalk would otherwise spell "Kilo" letter by letter. The disclosure lines were written for this tool; a native speaker should check them before they go on the air. Argos also has Korean, Thai, Hebrew and Indonesian packs, but no offline voice exists for them yet.

The operator stays in control

Six built-in rules
Keying is a physical act

Keying is a physical act

With review on (the default), the radio keys only after your own PTT press and a confirming press. The ID key sends your callsign directly. No timer or network request can key it.

Review before transmit

You see your words and the translation before anything goes out. Only Enter sends; any other key, or 20 s of silence, discards.

Station ID in English

FCC §97.119: your call in English phonetics on your first over, whenever 10 minutes would otherwise pass, and offered again when you quit. Nothing is sent on a timer. It refuses to transmit as N0CALL.

Disclosure on every over

"Machine-translated transmission", in their language, so nobody is misled about the synthetic voice.

Hard limits

45 s per recording, 90 s per transmission, and an unkey on every error, Ctrl-C, kill or dropped SSH session. Set the rig's own time-out timer too.

No voice cloning

Stock synthetic voices only. The box converts what you said; the only words it adds are the disclosure line and your ID.

Where it stands, and what is next

One step done
Five gates to go

The software works end to end and is tested, but it hasn't run on a Pi with the real models yet. Each step below has a pass/fail gate.

  • ✓
    Prototype built

    Tool, installer and 105 automated tests. Checked against real whisper.cpp, CTranslate2, Open JTalk and Hamlib. Code-reviewed, with the findings fixed.

  • Bench test on a Pi 4

    Gate: under 12 s of dead air for a 10 s over.

  • Practice mode with real speech

    Gate: callsigns, reports and Q-codes correct in 10 of 10 overs.

  • Into a dummy load

    Gate: clean audio with no ALC, ID on the first over, unkeys every time.

  • Receive a real Italian FM signal

    Gate: the meaning comes through on strong signals.

  • A full contact on simplex

    Gate: a complete QSO with a partner speaking the other language, no manual fixes.
