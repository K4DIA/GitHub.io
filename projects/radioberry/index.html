RadioBerry V2 Beta5
5 W portable HF radio

A self-contained HF software-defined radio in a Hammond instrument case: a Raspberry Pi 4 already on the shelf, a RadioBerry V2 Beta5 HAT, a 4.3 inch touchscreen, and a real weighted VFO knob because a radio you tune with a mouse is not a radio you want in the field. Three to five watts out, an internal battery, and a relay-selected harmonic filter bank.

Correction

The PA runs on 8.4 V, not 12 V. An earlier plan called for a 12 V PA regulator. That is wrong for this exact AURSINC board, whose listing allows roughly 6 to 8 V and recommends 8.4 V. Use a regulated 8.4 V rail, such as the Pololu D42V55F8. Do not put 12 to 13.8 V into it.

The three parts that decide the build

Key modules

RadioBerry V2

Perpetual beta

AURSINC or PA3GSB-compatible, preferably with the Cyclone 10LP 10CL025 FPGA. A direct-conversion SDR transceiver HAT for the Pi 4.

Published

  • AD9866 12-bit mixed-signal front end
  • Maximum receive bandwidth about 384 kHz
  • Bare transmit output about 10 dBm
  • Planning price $159.99

Watch for

  • Confirm the exact FPGA marking before loading gateware
  • Software images, gateware and GPIO behavior all need validating before permanent integration
  • A linear PA is required for any normal QRP output

Where

AURSINC product page

The 3 to 5 W PA

Biggest project risk

Sold as the AURSINC RadioBerry Preamp Board V2, which is a misleading name: it is a transmit power amplifier and receive routing board, not a receive preamplifier.

Published

  • 1 to 50 MHz, about 3 to 5 W out
  • SMA RF connections, SSB, AM and FM
  • Recommended supply 8.4 V, allowed roughly 6 to 8 V
  • Includes three SMA jumpers, stacking hardware, power lead
  • Listed at $78.99

Availability

  • AURSINC showed sold out during this research
  • Amazon showed the GOOZEEZOO version with no featured offer
  • A Newegg listing at about $131.99 carried contradictory compatibility information and is not recommended
  • Ask AURSINC directly for the 3 to 5 W, 6 to 8.4 V version

If it stays unavailable

  • About 10 dBm drive sensitivity
  • Linear 1 to 30 MHz, 3 to 5 W out
  • Hardware TX enable or bias control
  • RX bypass or external T/R switching
  • Stable into 50 ohms, adequately cooled

A substitute needs a separate T/R relay, sequenced PA enable, and confirmed compatibility with RadioBerry TX/RX timing. Do not pick one by advertised wattage. Do not pay the inflated Newegg price without written confirmation of Pi 4 and Beta5 compatibility.

The $80 filter bank

Settled

An assembled seven-position low-pass filter board, bought off eBay. It is the transmit harmonic filter and nothing else. It does not replace the PA.

Published

  • Seventh-order Chebyshev
  • 20 W continuous, 40 W at about 30 percent duty
  • 50 ohm SMA in and out
  • Under 0.5 dB insertion loss on all bands
  • Better than 35 dB return loss
  • 3.3 V or 5 V binary selection
  • Amidon T50 cores, Takamisawa relays, NP0 capacitors
  • 120 x 75 mm

What that costs you

At the worst stated 0.5 dB, 5 W in gives about 4.46 W out:

5 W × 10(−0.5/10) ≈ 4.46 W

It replaces the previously planned QRP Labs relay board, six individual filter modules, the relay driver assembly and all the interconnect wiring.

Before wiring

  • Get the seller's exact three-bit truth table
  • Do not assume the binary code order
  • Establish a safe default filter
  • Inhibit transmit while switching

Firmware rules for the filter bank

  1. Map the operating frequency to the right cutoff.
  2. Use the seller's truth table exactly.
  3. Disable PA bias and PTT before changing the relay address.
  4. Let the relays settle before enabling RF drive.
  5. If the software state is uncertain, select the next safer lower-frequency filter.
  6. Inhibit transmit if the requested band has no valid filter.
  7. Store the last valid filter state only if power-up stays safe.

The real VFO question

A knob, not a mouse

This design has a physical VFO. It needs no Si5351 or analog oscillator: the frequency synthesis lives inside the RadioBerry and the SDR software. The knob is an encoder that sends tuning commands to piHPSDR.

  • Bourns ENC1J-D28-L00128L optical quadrature encoder, 128 pulses per revolution, $54.50
  • A weighted 38 to 45 mm knob
  • A separate momentary VFO-select button, because the optical encoder has no push switch
  • Adafruit KB2040 RP2040 USB controller, $8.95
  • 74LVC14A Schmitt trigger for clean encoder edges
  • Short shielded USB-C to USB-A cable to the Pi

Three cheaper Bourns PEC11R encoders with integrated push switches handle AF gain, RF gain and the multifunction control. The RP2040 has enough I/O for the VFO, the encoders, the soft keys, the status outputs and the three filter address lines.

Two rails, one selector

Power architecture

Either the internal 12.8 V LiFePO4 or an external 13.8 V supply, chosen by a manual EXT-OFF-BAT switch. Manual selection stops the battery and the supply backfeeding each other.

Each source gets its own fuse. The selected source passes through reverse-polarity and transient protection, then splits into a filtered 5 V rail for the Pi, display, RadioBerry logic, RP2040, filter relays and audio, and a filtered 8.4 V rail for the PA alone.

Battery

Charging comes only from the dedicated 14.6 V LiFePO4 charger, with the radio switched off in the initial design. Do not use an ordinary lead-acid or lithium-ion charger.

Keeping the noise out of the receiver

RF and EMI practice

Layout

  1. Short RG-316 for every internal RF connection.
  2. Keep the PA, filter bank and SWR bridge together in the RF section.
  3. Bond the antenna connector directly to the metal rear panel.
  4. Aluminum partition between the RF section and the Pi, display and DC/DC section.
  5. Bonded shield cover over the switching regulators.

Wiring

  1. Keep DSI, USB, fan and audio wiring away from the PA input and the RadioBerry RX nodes.
  2. Ferrites on DSI, USB, fan and power leads where testing shows common-mode noise.
  3. One intentional chassis and RF ground strategy, not random multiple audio grounds.
  4. Sense PTT through an optocoupler or a similarly protected input, and keep the hardware TX-inhibit line independent of software where practical.
  5. Star washers or other gas-tight bonding on painted panels.

Cooling

  1. Temperature-control the fan if practical.
  2. Ensure continuous cooling for extended digital transmission.
  3. Forced air and verified bias for prolonged duty cycles.
  4. The listing mentions about 2.4 to 2.5 V bias for extended continuous transmit, but the actual board documentation governs.

Order of operations

Buy, then build, then test

Purchase order

  1. RadioBerry V2 Beta5, FPGA version confirmed.
  2. The PA, because availability is the biggest risk.
  3. The $80 filter board.
  4. The 4.3 in display.
  5. The enclosure, only after full-size templates confirm the layout.
  6. Optical VFO and RP2040 controller.
  7. Power modules, battery and charger.
  8. Audio hardware, connectors, switches, finishing parts.
  9. Custom PCBs last, after prototype wiring confirms the pin assignments.

Bring-up sequence

  1. Pi and RadioBerry on the bench, no PA.
  2. Confirm reception, gateware, piHPSDR, display and network.
  3. Verify the VFO and every front-panel input through the RP2040.
  4. Test all filter states at low level with the VNA. Record the real truth table.
  5. Verify the 5 V and 8.4 V rails into dummy loads before any radio board is connected.
  6. Connect the PA, confirm bias and enable sequencing into a 50 Ω dummy load.
  7. Minimum drive first. Check output power, current, stability.
  8. Sweep and confirm the filter for every supported band.
  9. Measure harmonics and spurious output with proper attenuation.
  10. Calibrate the forward and reflected detectors.
  11. Test microphone, PTT, speaker, headphones, CW keying.
  12. Thermal testing at the real voice and digital duty cycles.
  13. Repeat receiver noise testing with display, fan and DC/DC running.
  14. Only then, an antenna.

Commissioning equipment

Owned Excluded from the project cost.

  1. Current-limited 0 to 15 V, 5 A or larger bench supply.
  2. Digital multimeter.
  3. 50 Ω dummy load, 20 W continuous or better.
  4. Accurate low-power HF wattmeter.
  5. VNA covering HF to at least 100 MHz.
  6. Spectrum analyzer to at least 200 MHz.
  7. 3, 6, 10 and 20 dB attenuators with adequate power ratings.

Never connect the transmitter to an antenna until PA stability, filter selection and harmonic performance have been confirmed into a dummy load.

Where this lands

Bottom line

The $80 filter board is electrically well suited and is a cleaner answer than assembling the earlier QRP Labs filter system. The RadioBerry stays the exciter and receiver, a separate AURSINC-style board supplies the 3 to 5 W, and the filter bank removes transmit harmonics before the SWR bridge and the antenna.

The blocker is PA availability, not the filter board. Do not substitute an arbitrary HF amplifier until drive level, linearity, T/R switching and bias sequencing have all been verified.

With the Pi and the test equipment already on hand, plan on roughly $900 for the remaining parts and keep $1,000 available to absorb shipping, tax, PCB minimum quantities and small hardware. The microphone connector uses a project-specific pinout: label it clearly and do not imply compatibility with Icom, Yaesu, Kenwood or military handset wiring without an adapter.
