1.1Band Edges as Arithmetic, and Radio Afloat

At General you learned to keep your sideband inside the segment. The Extra pool makes you compute it:

  • A 3 kHz USB signal with the carrier at 14.348 MHz is illegal because the upper 1 kHz of the signal falls outside the 20-meter band (which ends at 14.350).
  • The lowest legal LSB carrier frequency is 3 kHz above the lower band edge, the General rule restated as a boundary condition.
  • The highest legal carrier for a 2.8 kHz USB data signal on 20 meters: the data segment ends at 14.150 MHz, so 14.1472 MHz puts the top of the signal exactly at the edge.
  • And the trap question: answering a CQ on 3.601 MHz LSB is a violation even for an Extra, because LSB extends downward and the sideband components fall below the 3.600 phone segment edge. Privileges do not repeal arithmetic.

The strange new bands come with unusual power units. On 2200 meters, maximum power is 1 watt EIRP; on 630 meters, 5 watts EIRP (except in parts of Alaska). EIRP, effective isotropic radiated power, instead of PEP, because at those wavelengths antennas are so inefficient that regulating radiated power is the only honest measure. On channelized 60-meter operation, a CW signal must be transmitted at the center frequency of the channel.

Radio goes to sea and sky with three rules: an amateur station aboard a ship or aircraft operates only with the approval of the master or pilot in command; the person in physical control must hold an FCC amateur license (or reciprocal authorization); and aboard a US-registered vessel in international waters, any FCC-issued amateur license suffices, because the vessel carries US jurisdiction with it.

One accountability rule closes the group: when a message forwarding system relays a rule-violating message, the party primarily accountable is the control operator of the originating station. The robots relay in good faith; the human who injected the message answers for it.

Exam Anchor (E1A) 14.348 USB is illegal: the upper 1 kHz exits the band. Lowest legal LSB carrier: 3 kHz above the lower edge. Highest 20-meter carrier for 2.8 kHz USB data: 14.1472 MHz. 3.601 LSB phone: sidebands extend below the segment. 2200 m: 1 W EIRP; 630 m: 5 W EIRP (Alaska excepted). 60-meter CW: at the channel center. Ship/aircraft: master or pilot approval; physical control by an FCC licensee; any FCC amateur license works on a US vessel in international waters. Forwarded violations: the originating station's control operator is accountable.

1.2Protected Places, Zoning, and RACES

Some locations impose duties on your station:

  • Within 1 mile of an FCC monitoring facility, you must protect the facility from harmful interference.
  • The National Radio Quiet Zone is the area surrounding the National Radio Astronomy Observatory, where radio silence is a scientific instrument.
  • On 70 centimeters, amateurs are secondary to federal radiolocation: if your repeater interferes with a radiolocation system, the control operator must cease operation or make changes that mitigate the interference.
  • Near a public-use airport, antenna structures may require FAA notification and FCC registration under Part 17, the airport-adjacent version of the General course's 200-foot rule.
  • If your clean signal still interferes with well-designed broadcast receivers, the FCC's remedy is proportionate: you may be required to avoid transmitting during certain hours on the offending frequencies, not to shut down.

PRB-1 returns with its scope stated precisely: it applies to state and local zoning regulations, and it requires reasonable accommodation of amateur radio. Note what it does not cover: private contracts like homeowner association covenants live outside PRB-1, which is why antenna-restricted subdivisions remain the hobby's sore subject.

Two definitions round out the regulatory vocabulary. A spurious emission is an emission outside the signal's necessary bandwidth that can be reduced or eliminated without affecting the information transmitted; that "without affecting the information" clause is what separates removable trash from your actual sidebands. And digital voice or SSTV on HF is held to a 3 kHz bandwidth, the same envelope as analog SSB.

RACES completes its three-course arc: stations operable under RACES rules are any FCC-licensed stations certified by the responsible civil defense organization, and a RACES station may use all amateur frequencies authorized to its control operator. RACES restricts who, not where.

Exam Anchor (E1B) 1 mile: FCC monitoring facility protection. National Radio Quiet Zone: surrounds the National Radio Astronomy Observatory. 70 cm radiolocation interference: cease or mitigate. Airport-adjacent structures: FAA notice and FCC Part 17 registration. Broadcast interference remedy: quiet hours on affected frequencies. PRB-1: state and local zoning must reasonably accommodate amateur radio. Spurious emission: outside the necessary bandwidth, removable without affecting the information. HF digital voice/SSTV: 3 kHz. RACES: civil-defense-certified stations, all frequencies authorized to the control operator.

1.3Automatic Control, Remote Control, and Crossing Borders

The machine rules first:

  • A station under automatic control may handle third-party communications only when transmitting RTTY or data emissions. The digital robots may relay; the voice robots may not.
  • A remotely controlled station whose control link fails may transmit for a maximum of 3 minutes. Every remote station is required to fail silent, and quickly.
  • Angle modulation below 29.0 MHz is capped at a modulation index of 1.0 at the highest modulation frequency, the rule that keeps HF FM narrow.
  • Spurious emissions below 30 MHz must be at least 43 dB below the fundamental. That is the number your "clean signal" obligation resolves to.

The low bands carry a unique pre-flight requirement: before transmitting on 630 or 2200 meters, operators must notify the Utilities Technology Council (UTC) with their call sign and station coordinates, then wait 30 days; if not told the station is within 1 kilometer of power-line carrier systems, operation may begin. Those bands share spectrum with the signaling the electric grid uses on its own lines, hence the unusual courtesy call. One friendly detail: phone is permitted on the entire 630-meter band.

Crossing borders, three instruments:

  • CEPT lets FCC licensees operate in many European countries and vice versa; the requirement when operating under CEPT is carrying a copy of FCC Public Notice DA 16-1048.
  • An IARP is the permit for US amateurs operating in certain countries of the Americas.
  • Communications with foreign stations are limited to those incidental to the purposes of the amateur service and remarks of a personal character, the international restatement of the noncommercial deal from your first course.

Exam Anchor (E1C) Automatic control plus third-party traffic: RTTY or data only. Remote control link failure: 3-minute maximum transmission. Modulation index limit below 29 MHz: 1.0. Spurious below 30 MHz: -43 dB. 630/2200 m: notify UTC (call sign and coordinates), wait 30 days, clear unless within 1 km of PLC systems; 60-meter data bandwidth 2.8 kHz; phone on all of 630 m. CEPT: Europe, carry DA 16-1048. IARP: the Americas. Foreign communications: incidental to the service plus personal remarks.

1.4Space Stations and the Machines Aloft

The space rules, finally yours to use in full:

Definitions first. Telemetry is one-way transmission of measurements at a distance from the measuring instrument, the satellite reporting its health. A space telecommand station transmits communications to initiate, modify, or terminate the functions of a space station, the ground steering the bird. And the encryption exception you have waited three courses for: telecommand signals from a space telecommand station may be encrypted, the one place obscured meaning is legal, because satellite control channels must not be hijackable.

Where satellites may transmit. Space station allocations exist on HF at 40, 20, 15, and 10 meters; on VHF at 2 meters only; on UHF at 70 centimeters and 13 centimeters. Ray's mnemonic for VHF: 6 meters and 1.25 meters never got the satellite invitation.

Who does what. Telecommand stations are any amateur stations designated by the space station licensee. Earth stations, the ground half of any satellite contact, may be operated by any amateur, subject to the control operator's license privileges, which is why your Technician students can work satellites the week they pass. And the exhaustive list of stations allowed one-way transmissions: space stations, beacon stations, and telecommand stations.

Closer to the ground:

  • A balloon-borne telemetry station's identification requires its call sign, like every other amateur transmitter.
  • A station operated by telecommand on or within 50 km of Earth (the remote bases and repeaters of the world) must have posted at the station: a photocopy of the license, plus labels with the name, address, and phone of both the licensee and the control operator. All of these.
  • Operating a model craft by telecommand: maximum 1 watt transmitter output. Your RC aircraft on amateur frequencies flies on a single watt.

Exam Anchor (E1D) Telemetry: one-way measurement transmission. Space telecommand station: initiates, modifies, or terminates space station functions; its telecommand signals may be encrypted. Space station allocations: HF 40/20/15/10 m; VHF 2 m only; UHF 70 cm and 13 cm. Telecommand stations: designated by the space station licensee. Earth stations: any amateur within their privileges. One-way transmissions: space, beacon, and telecommand stations. Balloon telemetry ID: call sign. Telecommand station posting: license photocopy plus licensee and control operator labels (all). Model craft telecommand: 1 watt.

1.5The Other Side of the Table

The VE program, from the administrator's chair you are about to occupy.

The structure: a Volunteer Examiner Coordinator is an organization that has entered into an agreement with the FCC to coordinate, prepare, and administer amateur exams. The VECs maintain the question pools for all US amateur exams, which is why this course keeps citing NCVEC. Accreditation is not automatic and not from the FCC: a VEC confirms that the applicant meets the FCC requirements to serve.

Money, barely: VEs and VECs may be reimbursed only for out-of-pocket expenses of preparing, processing, administering, and coordinating examinations. Teaching license classes is noble and unreimbursable under Part 97.

Running the session:

  • Each administering VE is responsible for the proper conduct and necessary supervision of the session. Responsibility does not delegate to the session manager; all three of you own it.
  • A candidate who fails to comply with instructions gets their examination immediately terminated. No warnings phase; the rule assumes the instructions were clear the first time.
  • You may not administer an exam to your relatives as listed in the FCC rules. Elmer your nephew all year; someone else examines him.
  • Paperwork by outcome: a failed applicant gets their application document returned to them. A passing candidate's session ends with three VEs certifying qualification and compliance, and the documents going to the coordinating VEC according to its instructions, never directly to the FCC.
  • The penalty for fraudulent administration or certification: revocation of the VE's station license grant and suspension of their operator license. The program runs on trust and enforces it with everything you own.

Exam Anchor (E1E) VEC: FCC-agreement organization coordinating exams; VECs maintain the question pools; a VEC confirms VE qualifications. Reimbursement: exam preparation, processing, administration, coordination only. Each administering VE is responsible for session conduct. Non-compliant candidate: immediate termination. No examining your FCC-listed relatives. Failed applicant: application returned. Passing: three VEs certify; documents go to the coordinating VEC per its instructions. Fraud: station license revocation and operator license suspension.

1.6Amplifiers, Line A, and the Odds and Ends

The last group is Part 97's miscellaneous drawer, and every item eventually matters to somebody:

  • Spread spectrum is permitted only above 222 MHz (and, from your General course, at 10 watts PEP).
  • External RF amplifiers below 144 MHz normally require FCC certification to be sold by dealers, with one exception the exam highlights: an amplifier constructed or modified by an amateur for use at an amateur station may be sold without certification. Homebrew remains blessed. The certification standard itself: the amplifier must satisfy the spurious emission standards when operated at the lesser of 1500 watts or its full output.
  • Line A is a line roughly parallel to and south of the US-Canadian border. North of it, in the contiguous states, amateurs may not transmit in 420 to 430 MHz, protecting Canadian systems in that segment. Geography is a band condition.
  • Visiting Canadian amateurs operate in the US under the terms of their Canadian license, not to exceed US Extra class privileges, the reciprocal courtesy running both directions across Line A.
  • A Special Temporary Authority (STA) exists to provide for experimental amateur communications, the FCC's mechanism for blessing the genuinely new.
  • The business rule, stated with Extra precision: an amateur may send a message to a business only when neither the amateur nor their employer has a pecuniary interest in the communication, and the prohibited-communications list is headed by transmissions for hire or material compensation.
  • Mesh networks inherit the whole rulebook: messages encoded to obscure their meaning cannot travel an amateur mesh, no matter how internet-shaped the network feels.
  • An auxiliary station (the links and remote receivers gluing repeater systems together) may have as control operator any Technician class or higher licensee.

Exam Anchor (E1F) Spread spectrum: above 222 MHz only. Uncertified amplifier sale: legal when amateur-built or amateur-modified; certification requires meeting spurious standards at the lesser of 1500 W or full output. Line A: roughly parallel to and south of the Canadian border; no 420-430 MHz north of it. Canadian visitors: Canadian terms, capped at Extra privileges. STA: experimental amateur communications. Business messages: only with no pecuniary interest for amateur or employer; for-hire communications prohibited. Mesh networks: no meaning-obscuring encoding. Auxiliary station control operator: Technician or higher.

Tip A mesh network follows the same rule as any ham link: nothing encoded to obscure its meaning.

E1F10 Who may be the control operator of an auxiliary station?

Answer Only Technician, General, Advanced, or Amateur Extra class operators

Tip An auxiliary station control operator must be Technician, General, Advanced, or Extra, but never Novice.

E1F11 Which of the following best describes one of the standards that must be met by an external RF power amplifier if it is to qualify for a grant of FCC certification?

Answer It must satisfy the FCC’s spurious emission standards when operated at the lesser of 1500 watts or its full output power

Tip To earn FCC certification, an external amp must meet the spurious emission limits at the lesser of 1500 watts or full output.
