Extra Course, Module 9: Operating at the Frontier

Covers NCVEC 2024-2028 Extra Pool, Subelement E2 (Operating Procedures). Five questions on your exam come from this material.

Ray hands you a printed satellite schedule, a contest calendar, and a moonrise table. "Extra operating is the stuff with procedures attached: linear transponders, television, pileups, moonbounce, and the machine modes at their limits. None of it is harder than what you already do. 9.1Satellites, the Full Manual

The Technician course flew FM satellites; the Extra pool flies the linear birds.

Orbital vocabulary: an ascending pass travels south to north. Keplerian elements remain the parameters defining the orbit. A geostationary satellite appears fixed in one position in the sky, the always-available bird that changed amateur satellite work. And a satellite's mode designates its uplink and downlink frequency bands, the letters specifying frequency ranges: L band and S band are the 23- and 13-centimeter bands, extending the U/V vocabulary upward.

The linear transponder is the heart of the serious birds: it relays a whole band segment at once, and it accepts SSB, CW, FM, SSTV, PSK, and packet alike. All of these; a linear transponder does not care what rides through it. The inverting variety processes the uplink by mixing it with a local oscillator and transmitting the difference product, with three consequences that all appear as one "all of these" answer: the band segment comes out reversed, USB becomes LSB, and Doppler shift is reduced because uplink and downlink shifts run in opposite directions and partially cancel.

Etiquette, quantified from the Technician course: uplink ERP must be limited on a linear transponder because the transponder's total downlink power is shared: your excess uplink reduces the downlink power available to every other user. The beacon comparison from your first course was this rule's field method.

Hardware craft: a circularly polarized antenna minimizes both spin modulation and Faraday rotation, Module 2's EME answer doing double duty for satellites. And the digital store-and-forward function holds digital messages in the satellite for later download, the orbiting mailbox that serves stations that never share a footprint.

Exam Anchor (E2A) Ascending pass: south to north. Keplerian elements: orbit parameters. Geostationary: fixed position in the sky. Mode letters: uplink and downlink frequency ranges; L and S bands are 23 and 13 cm. Linear transponders relay SSB, CW, FM, SSTV, PSK, packet (all). Inverting transponder: LO mixing, difference product out; band reversed, sidebands inverted, Doppler reduced (all). Limit uplink ERP: excess robs downlink power from other users. Circular polarization: counters spin modulation and Faraday rotation. Store-and-forward: holds messages for later download.

9.2Pictures Through the Air

Amateur television, both speeds:

Fast-scan (analog NTSC): a frame contains 525 horizontal lines, painted as an interlaced pattern: odd-numbered lines in one field, even-numbered lines in the next, halving flicker without doubling bandwidth. The bandwidth diet that makes a 6 MHz TV channel possible at all is vestigial sideband: amplitude modulation transmitting one complete sideband and only a portion of the other, which reduces bandwidth while preserving the fidelity of low-frequency video components, the parts of the picture that need both sidebands most. The practical 70-centimeter trick: fast-scan ATV transmits on frequencies shared with cable TV channels, so an ordinary analog cable receiver becomes an ATV monitor.

Digital ATV speaks broadcast: DVB-T uses QAM and QPSK modulation (Module 6's constellations, employed), and its coding rate states the FEC budget: a rate of 3/4 means one quarter of the transmitted data is forward error correction. Redundancy as a line item.

Slow-scan trades motion for bandwidth, fitting pictures into a voice channel:

  • Brightness is encoded as tone frequency: higher tone, brighter pixel, the image literally sung.
  • Color is sent as sequential lines, red, green, and blue scans of each line in turn.
  • Specific tone frequencies signal the start of each new picture line, and the VIS code at the transmission's head identifies the SSTV mode in use, so the software knows how to decode what follows.
  • Digital SSTV using the DRM protocol is received on an ordinary SSB receiver, the sound card doing the rest.

Exam Anchor (E2B) NTSC: 525 lines; interlace: odd lines one field, even the next. Vestigial sideband: one full sideband plus part of the other; preserves low-frequency video fidelity at reduced bandwidth. 70 cm ATV: shares cable TV channel frequencies. DVB-T: QAM and QPSK; coding rate 3/4 = 25 percent FEC. SSTV: brightness as tone frequency; color as sequential lines; new lines signaled by specific tones; VIS code identifies the mode; DRM SSTV needs only an SSB receiver.

9.3Contests, DX, and the Remote Station

The competitive arts, formalized:

  • Identification in a pileup: send your full call sign, once or twice. The partial-call habit wastes everyone's time and the pool says so directly.
  • Why DX stations work split (transmit and receive on different frequencies): it separates the callers from the DX station, improves efficiency, and accommodates callers who cannot transmit on the DX station's frequency. All of these; split is not affectation, it is traffic engineering. The dual-VFO control from General Module 8 was built for this moment.
  • Where and where not: VHF/UHF contest activity concentrates in the weak-signal segment near the calling frequency, and contesting is excluded from 30 meters by band-plan custom, the quiet band from your General course staying quiet even on contest weekends.
  • Paperwork with formats: contest logs are submitted in Cabrillo format; general log data is exchanged as ADIF (the format your General capstone already used); confirmations flow through Logbook of The World, which accepts US-to-US, international, and award-credit contacts alike. All of these. A DX QSL Manager handles the receiving and sending of confirmations on a DX station's behalf, the administrative Elmer of the DX world.
  • The remote station, resolved: operating a US station remotely, with the transmitter in the US, requires no additional identifier. And the physics of remote operation gets its word: latency, the delay between a control operator's action and the corresponding change in the transmitted signal, the reason remote CW takes practice.
  • Mesh networking, completed: amateur mesh networks run on frequencies shared with unlicensed wireless data services, built from wireless routers running custom firmware, the Technician course's modified-firmware fact now paired with its spectrum.

Exam Anchor (E2C) Pileup ID: full call, once or twice. DX split: separates callers, improves efficiency, accommodates privilege differences (all). VHF contest activity: weak-signal segment near the calling frequency. Contesting excluded from 30 meters. Cabrillo: contest log submission. ADIF: log exchange. LoTW: confirms all contact types (all). QSL Manager: handles a DX station's confirmations. US remote transmitter: no added indicator. Latency: control-to-transmission delay. Mesh: unlicensed-shared frequencies, custom-firmware routers.

9.4Bouncing Signals Off the Sky

The VHF/UHF digital frontier, where Module 2's exotic paths get their operating procedures:

  • Meteor scatter runs on MSK144, the WSJT-X mode built for split-second trail reflections. EME runs on Q65, and the pool distinguishes it from its ancestor precisely: Q65 averages multiple receive cycles, accumulating a signal too weak for any single period, which is how amateurs with modest arrays now work the Moon. JT65, the ancestor, contributed the family traits: multitone AFSK modulation decoding at very low signal-to-noise ratios.
  • The EME contact method is the family discipline: time-synchronous transmissions alternating between stations, each station owning fixed periods, the FT8 rhythm from your General course stretched to fit a two-and-a-half-second lunar echo.
  • In VHF contests using FT8 or FT4, the exchange replaces the signal report with the grid square, the Technician course's grid locator now serving as contest currency.
  • APRS completes its arc with protocol detail: it rides AX.25 packet, transmitting its beacons as Unnumbered Information frames (fire-and-forget, no connection), relayed by digipeaters. The path WIDE3-1 decodes as three digipeater hops requested, one remaining, the packet's travel budget written in its header. And APRS remains the technology for real-time tracking of balloons carrying amateur transmitters, Module 1's balloon rules now with their tracking layer.

Exam Anchor (E2D) MSK144: meteor scatter. Q65: EME, averaging multiple receive cycles. JT65: multitone AFSK, very low SNR decoding. EME method: time-synchronous alternating transmissions. FT8/FT4 VHF contests: grid square replaces the report. APRS: AX.25 protocol, Unnumbered Information frames, digipeater relay; WIDE3-1 = three hops requested, one remaining; APRS tracks amateur balloons.

9.5HF Digital at the Limits

The HF machine modes, specified to Extra depth:

  • Data emissions below 30 MHz use FSK, and the direct variety modulates the transmitter's VFO itself, versus audio FSK's tones through the SSB chain, the General course's RTTY distinction now stated as architecture.
  • The WSJT-X family tree: transmit/receive timing is synchronized by computer clocks (the whole family's shared metronome); FT8's cycle is 15 seconds; FT4's name decodes as four-tone continuous-phase frequency shift keying; and FST4 is the flexible sibling: four-tone Gaussian FSK, variable transmit/receive periods, multiple tone spacings. All of these. Longer periods buy deeper decodes, the Q65 averaging principle available on HF.
  • The taxonomy questions: WSPR does not support keyboard-to-keyboard operation (it is a beacon, full stop, from General Module 6); PSK31 uses variable-length character coding (the Varicode from General); PACTOR transfers binary files where the keyboard modes cannot; FT8 has the narrowest bandwidth of the common contenders; and under clear conditions PACTOR IV delivers the highest data throughput. Every mode is a point on the same curve: bandwidth and time traded against data and depth.
  • The last machine standing is ALE, automatic link establishment: ALE stations constantly scan a list of frequencies, activating the radio when their call sign arrives on any of them. It is Module 2's propagation problem solved by brute diligence, the radio finding its own open band, and it is how interoperable HF networks stay connected across the day without a human tuning anything.

Exam Anchor (E2E) Sub-30 MHz data: FSK; direct FSK modulates the VFO. WSJT-X timing: computer clock synchronization. FT8: 15-second cycles. FT4: four-tone continuous-phase FSK. FST4: Gaussian four-tone, variable periods, multiple spacings (all). WSPR: not keyboard-to-keyboard. PSK31: variable-length coding. PACTOR: binary file transfer; PACTOR IV: highest clear-channel throughput. FT8: narrowest of the common modes. ALE: scans a frequency list, activating on its call sign.
